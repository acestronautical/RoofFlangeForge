Roof Flange Forge

Generate a printable flange adapter for a ribbed sheet-metal roof.

How this tool works

This app generates printable flange adapters whose underside is shaped to conform to a periodic ribbed sheet-metal roof, so the printed part sits flush against the roof rather than rocking on the rib peaks.

Workflow

  1. Pick a Shape — circular flange, rectangular flange, mounting strip, or a roof-profile preview.
  2. Pick a Roof profile — trapezoidal ribs (most stamped body panels) or corrugated (sinusoidal, like barn tin).
  3. Match the Roof dimensions to the actual metal you're mounting on. Measure a rib peak-to-peak with calipers and split it into rib width and indent widths.
  4. Choose Center on: Rib or Indent depending on where the flange center lands on the periodic pattern; and Topside vs Underside for the side of the panel it mounts on.
  5. Hit Render, review the 3D preview, then Download.

Topside vs. underside

A single adapter is really two mating parts split along the roof surface: the topside sits above the roof with pads that drop into the indents, and the underside is a mirror piece that receives those pads from below. Print both, sandwich the sheet metal between them, and bolt through.

Print & fitment tolerance

The generated STL matches the nominal roof profile exactly. Real prints and real sheet metal are noisier, so you'll almost always want to bake in some clearance:

  • Loose over the ribs: nudge Indent depth up by 0.2–0.4 mm and Indent width at floor up by a similar amount so the adapter drops onto the ribs without forcing.
  • Snug pad-in-indent fit: pull Indent width at top down by 0.1–0.3 mm.
  • Sheet-metal thickness: this controls the vertical gap between the topside and underside pieces — add 0.1–0.2 mm on top of the real gauge to account for paint, primer, and print squish.
  • Corner radius at rib bends: real stamped panels have rounded transitions; too-sharp corners on the adapter will lift it off the plateau. Match this to the real bend radius on the panel, or slightly larger.

Everything is parametric — no need to guess. Print a small test coupon (a short strip that spans one rib), iterate on the tolerance values, then run the full flange when the coupon slips on cleanly.
